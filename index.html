01 / Research

From numerical methods to galaxy clusters

My research uses large cosmological simulations to study galaxies, clusters and the large-scale structure of the Universe. A central question is how cooling, star formation and feedback shape the matter we observe.

Cosmological simulations

I co-developed the HYDRA gravity and hydrodynamics code and contributed to the Virgo Consortium, Millennium Gas, GIMIC and The Three Hundred project.

Cluster baryon physics

Cooling, entropy and feedback connect numerical models to X-ray and Sunyaev–Zeldovich observations. My work also follows the assembly of brightest cluster galaxies and intra-cluster light.

Methods and reproducibility

Comparisons of halo finders, subhalo finders and merger trees test what our analysis tools actually measure, and how reliably results can be compared across simulations.

02 / Software & experiments

Small seeds. Larger possibilities.

Alongside simulation research, I build software experiments and frameworks for testing computational methods.

TimeLord

Experiments in compact, self-contained payloads: how much behaviour and structure can be packed into a small seed and reconstructed from it?

AI benchmarking

Research problems need measurable definitions of success. I am developing benchmark frameworks for AI-assisted scientific work, including lunar crater detection and halo finding.

Open scientific software

Numerical innovation is most useful when others can inspect, test and reuse it. My interests include robust analysis tools, comparison datasets and reproducible workflows.

03 / Teaching

Research as the classroom

Real data, shared code and explicit tests.

My imaging and data processing teaching uses lunar crater finding as a team research project. Students build and evaluate a complete pipeline, using GitHub, code review and reproducible benchmarks.

Generative AI is part of the workflow. The emphasis is on understanding the problem, testing the method and justifying the result.
